\IEEEPARstart{S}{urgical} phase recognition (SPR), which identifies frame-wise surgical intentions from procedural videos, is foundational for intelligent operating rooms by supporting intraoperative decision support~\cite{vermeulen2023ultra}, real-time anomaly detection~\cite{mithany2023advancements}, and postoperative skill assessment~\cite{cao2023intelligent}. 
Recently, the domain has shifted toward pre-training surgical foundation models (FMs) on expansive corpora~\cite{batic2024endovit,yang2026large,wu2026surgmotion}, which demonstrate promising performance. 
The FM paradigm itself, however, carries two deficiencies that loom large in clinical practice. The first is its reliance on massive training data: curating expansive surgical corpora incurs prohibitive annotation costs, privacy risks, and procedural biases. The second is its inability to run in real time: the parameter explosion that accompanies scaling renders heavy architectures computationally infeasible for resource-constrained clinical hardware, while practical SPR demands real-time inference, as instant intraoperative feedback directly impacts patient safety. Consequently, overcoming the data hunger and the deployment latency of the FM paradigm remains a critical yet overlooked challenge for practical SPR.

The data deficiency, we argue, can be sidestepped rather than satisfied: generic visual FMs already provide rich pretrained representations, and our systematic evaluation across five surgical datasets reveals that they match or outperform specialized surgical FMs in few-shot scenarios, despite lacking any surgical pretraining exposure (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp:fm_analysis}). We therefore build on a generic visual prior and propose SPR-Teacher, a redundancy-aware spatiotemporal adaptation framework that slashes the annotation requirement of SPR from massive corpora to a handful of labeled videos. Surgical videos exhibit a dual redundancy: background regions dominate the field, and steady-state frames repeat within each phase, diluting the already scarce supervision. Salient spatial token selection (SSTS) and keyframe-prior temporal fusion (KPTF) turn this redundancy into an inductive bias, filtering irrelevant background while preserving discriminative moments for effective video understanding. Consequently, SPR-Teacher breaks the FM paradigm's reliance on massive training data.

Although SPR-Teacher breaks the data reliance of the FM paradigm, it still inherits the second deficiency: as a heavy ViT, it incurs substantial computational overhead and rigid deployment constraints. To cure the real-time deficiency, we propose an architecture-agnostic distillation strategy that transfers the teacher into SPR-Lite. To bridge the teacher-student structural discrepancy, a cross-attention projection (CAP) maps heterogeneous student feature maps into the teacher's token space, enabling flexible supervision across CNN, ViT, and hybrid architectures. Complementing this alignment, a spatiotemporally-guided distillation (STGD) transfers spatial and temporal priors into intermediate student representations, supplying the dense supervisory signals missing under scarce annotations. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}, select SPR-Lite students drawn from three diverse backbone families exceed the 25\,FPS real-time threshold on a single CPU core while approaching teacher-level accuracy, successfully reconciling accuracy and speed for clinical deployment.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item We propose a redundancy-aware spatiotemporal adaptation framework
    for few-shot SPR: SSTS compresses spatial redundancy via a soft top-$K$
    saliency gate and KPTF compresses temporal redundancy via keyframe-prior
    fusion, adapting a frozen image-level foundation model to surgical
    video with only a handful of labeled videos.
    \item We propose an architecture-agnostic distillation scheme that
    compresses the adapted model into lightweight students of arbitrary
    backbone families: CAP projects heterogeneous feature maps into the
    teacher's token space, and STGD transfers the teacher's where- and
    when-to-look priors as dense supervision to compensate for scarce
    labels, enabling real-time CPU inference.
    \item Extensive experiments on four public and one private datasets show
    that our method surpasses prior state-of-the-art by large margins under
    few-shot settings, while the distilled Lite models approach this accuracy
    with under 9M parameters at over 25\,FPS on a single CPU core.
\end{enumerate}
