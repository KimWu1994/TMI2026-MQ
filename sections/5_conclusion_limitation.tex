Surgical phase recognition is a fundamental task for intelligent operating
rooms, and its clinical deployment hinges on two challenges. The first is the
scarcity of expert labels, which makes few-shot recognition attractive. In
this regime, a simple comparison of generic and surgical foundation models
shows that DINOv3, although barely exposed to surgical data, surpasses
surgical FMs, so we adapt it to surgical videos through a spatiotemporal
tuning scheme, yielding DINO-SPR. The second is real-time inference, which the
ViT backbone of DINO-SPR cannot meet, so we distill it into lightweight
students of arbitrary architectures, obtaining DINO-SPR Lite. Across four
public and one private dataset, DINO-SPR surpasses prior methods under
few-shot settings, while DINO-SPR Lite retains this performance with under 9M
parameters and exceeds the 25\,FPS on a single CPU,
offering a practical route toward low-annotation, real-time deployment.
Nevertheless, several limitations remain. First, we only consider foundation
models with publicly released
weights, so the coverage may not be exhaustive. Second, surgical FMs
inevitably pre-train on public surgical videos, part of which may overlap with
our evaluation data and inflate their scores, making the FM comparison not
perfectly fair. Third, real-time efficiency is measured on a server CPU core
rather than on clinical hardware, where quantization and further engineering
optimization remain to be explored. We leave these for future work.
