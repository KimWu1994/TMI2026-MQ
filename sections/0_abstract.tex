\begin{abstract}
While surgical phase recognition (SPR) is advancing into the foundation model (FM) era, practical progress remains constrained by the difficulty of collecting massive clinical training data and the growing computational overhead of expanding model scales. Intriguingly, our systematic evaluation of representative FMs under few-shot SPR reveals that the generic FM DINOv3 outperforms specialized surgical FMs. To overcome these dual constraints, we present a unified solution based on the generic FM. First, we introduce DINO-SPR, a spatiotemporal adaptation tuning framework that unlocks the potent generalization capabilities of DINOv3 for the surgical domain using only a handful of labeled videos. By incorporating salient spatial token selection (SSTS) and keyframe-prior temporal fusion (KPTF), DINO-SPR effectively filters spatial and temporal redundancies in surgical videos under scarce annotations. Second, to meet real-time deployment requirements, we propose DINO-SPR Lite, an architecture-agnostic distillation strategy that compresses DINO-SPR into lightweight student models across arbitrary backbone families to suit diverse clinical hardware deployments. Comprehensive experiments on five SPR datasets, including four public benchmarks and one private clinical cohort, demonstrate that DINO-SPR substantially outperforms state-of-the-art methods in few-shot scenarios, and DINO-SPR Lite achieves real-time CPU inference exceeding 25\,FPS with under 9 million model parameters.
\end{abstract}
